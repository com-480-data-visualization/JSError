Project Overview

Understanding European Diversity Through Data

Mission Statement

The origins of religion can be traced back to at least 100,000 years ago, and it has continuously and profoundly influenced the development and continuation of human civilization. Our project aims to use data visualization to display the distribution, evolution, and societal impact of global religious patterns, specifically showcasing European data through interactive visualizations.

Key Features
  • Interactive European map with multiple data layers
  • Religious denomination temporal analysis
  • Country comparison tools
  • Economic perception visualization
  • Social attitude exploration
  • Responsive design across devices
Data Categories
  • 🛐 Religious beliefs and belonging
  • 💰 Economic perception
  • 💑 Marriage patterns
  • 🏥 Abortion attitudes
  • 🏳️‍🌈 Same-sex marriage support
  • 📊 Demographic information

Dataset

Pew Research Center European Surveys

Data Sources

All datasets used in this project are sourced from the Pew Research Center, a nonpartisan fact tank that informs the public about issues, attitudes and trends shaping the world.

Western Europe Survey

Period: April to August 2017

Sample: 26,000+ telephone interviews

Coverage: 16 countries, 14 languages

Focus: Role of religion in public and private life, views toward religious minorities

Central & Eastern Europe Survey

Period: June 2015 to July 2016

Sample: 31,000+ face-to-face interviews

Coverage: 19 countries, 17 languages

Focus: Religion in public life and national identity post-Soviet Union

Highlight

For our color scheme we chose a purple gradient with blue. As religion and various opinions are very abstract topics, we wanted something neutral but engaging. The smooth gradient coloring is kept consistent throughout all visualizations.

Website Layout

From the start, we focused on country comparisons with the map as our centerpiece. We chose to color countries according to their most common responses for immediate surface-level insights.

  • Scrollable fullPage.js navigation
  • Glassmorphism design elements
  • Responsive grid layouts
  • Interactive tooltips and legends
User Experience

Our goal was to make data exploration as smooth and intuitive as possible. Users can seamlessly navigate between different data perspectives and compare countries interactively.

  • Hover interactions for data exploration
  • Compare mode for country analysis
  • Temporal slider for time-based data
  • Emoji-based category selection

Storytelling

Guiding Users Through European Diversity

We wanted to tell the story of how religions impact the way people perceive their countries and what beliefs they hold. However, during our exploration, we found that religions do not divide people as much as initially thought. We wanted users to experience this same realization through various topics such as economic state perception and social attitudes.

User Journey
1. Introduction & Expectations
We start by introducing users to the available visualizations and pose leading questions that create expectations and act as hooks for deeper exploration.
2. Mission & Context
We explain our mission to show European diversity and the endless comparison possibilities, providing context about data categories and processing methods.
3. Interactive Exploration
Users explore the interactive map by selecting categories and hovering over countries, with the option to compare two countries for deeper visual understanding.
4. Temporal Analysis
The temporal visualization reveals how religious denominations have evolved across Europe from 2002 to 2024, providing historical context.

Challenges

Technical and Design Obstacles

Dataset Selection
Initial challenge was finding standardized datasets across different countries. Census data varied significantly between nations and didn't capture opinions and views. We settled on Pew Research Center datasets for their comprehensive coverage and standardized methodology.
Data Integration
Finding shared data across both Western and Eastern European datasets proved challenging. We had to filter, clean, and process aggregated survey responses while maintaining data integrity and comparability.
Geographic Mapping
Misalignment between country names in our datasets and topological data caused visualization issues. Some countries appeared on the map but showed no data due to naming inconsistencies that required manual mapping solutions.
Bias and Data Selection
Choosing which survey data to represent required careful consideration of our own biases and interests. We focused on data potentially impacted by religious beliefs: abortion rights, same-sex marriage, and marriage status.
Temporal Data Processing
Creating the temporal visualization required processing multiple years of data with varying country coverage and question formats. Initial loading issues were resolved by implementing proper data loading sequences and SVG rendering timing.
